What a CV doesn't tell

A CV lists roles; it doesn't say what happened inside them. I started on a warehouse floor, not behind a dashboard. Each role added a layer, operations, ERP, BI, automation, data, AI, but the logic never changed: understand the field first, tool it second. Here are the four missions that count, told in full.

Oct 2025 → presentFranprix · Casino Group · food retail

The system said everything was fine

RoleSenior Business Analyst, Supply Chain Performance
FieldSupply data chain
StackSnowflake, Make, Python, LLM

I joined a data chain that was already running. Reporting in place, a Snowflake warehouse, a fleet of automations in production. My role fit in two words: harden and tool. The real work started when I looked under the hood.

What I found

The warehouse documentation listed 4 schemas. There were 24. The fleet held 159 automations, but nobody measured how many actually held in production. And indicators went out in weekly reporting with nothing verifying they were correct. The common thread across all three: a system that displays calm while the data says otherwise.

What I did

I mapped the warehouse from scratch, 24 schemas and the lineage of about 65 views rebuilt by hand, into a reference that became the source of truth for the chain. I diagnosed the automation fleet after an API migration: 5,376 failed executions listed one by one, then 27,625 € of financial risk traced line by line and prioritised. I traced a false service indicator to its root cause, a backup table frozen since July 2022 where 83 to 85 % of rows silently fell back. I industrialised a steering newsletter, cut from 4 hours to 15 minutes per edition. And I analysed 37.8 M€ of inventory through the SLOB method.

Only once that base was hardened did I wire AI onto it: an MCP PoC and a text-to-SQL assistant, with read-only access, validation and guardrails. The hand-built reference became the context that stops the model from inventing tables.

Before wiring an LLM to the warehouse, I had to know what the warehouse actually held. AI didn't fix the system; it came after. What I take from it

The result

−94 % weekly reporting time37.8 M€ inventory analysed159 scenarios administered27,625 € of risk traced

Oct 2024 → Oct 2025Samsung Electronics · consumer electronics

15 million euros a week, reporting by hand

RoleSupply Chain Analyst
Scope~15 M€ weekly flows
StackSAP ERP, Python, VBA

Steering roughly 15 million euros of flows a week, between the European teams and the Korean HQ. At that scale, a measurement error is not recovered; it propagates.

What I found

A time-consuming supply report, produced largely by hand, and KPIs whose reliability depended on whoever compiled them. Stock discrepancies that cost money without a clear source.

What I did

I replaced manual work with Python and VBA dashboards, and hardened the supply KPIs on SAP ERP. The work was not only technical: coordinating Europe and Korea means making data a common language when the context is not.

A number shared across two continents must mean the same thing on both sides. That is as much a definition problem as a calculation one. What I take from it

The result

−20 % stock discrepancies−50 % reporting time~15 M€ weekly flows piloted
Sep 2023 → Sep 2024Oplit · SaaS vendor · industry

Twelve clients, four industries, one product

RoleMOA Project Manager, production planning
Scope12 strategic clients
SectorsAerospace, defence, luxury, steel

Deploying a production planning solution at twelve clients that share nothing: aerospace, defence, luxury, steel. The product was the same. The constraints, never.

What I did

My MOA role was a translation role: turn a business need into product configuration without betraying either. I wrote the specifications per industry, ran the cross-company API integrations, and made sure each deployment matched the client's reality rather than the product brochure.

This is where my work started shifting towards product: understanding that the same tool does not deploy the same way depending on the ground, and that value lives in that gap. That product dimension is one I am still building; this role was its concrete starting point.

The same product does not solve the same problem across industries. The MOA job is holding both ends without lying to anyone. What I take from it

The result

12 clients deployed−15 % process cost−25 % lead time
