The supplier that vanished from the report

A major supplier vanished from the service-level report for two weeks. No number, no alert. The calculation was right. The definition had a hole.

A supplier that stops delivering entirely does not drag the indicator down: it drops out of it. The worst possible case was, by design, the only invisible one.

Investigation

From an absence to a hole in the definition

01 · Symptom

An absence, not a drop

The upstream service-level report no longer shows a major supplier. The first reflex would have been to suspect a display bug. The second, more useful one: go back to the source and redo the calculation row by row, outside the BI tool.

02 · Rule

Reread the definition, not just the code

Service level = quantity delivered / quantity ordered, computed only on received or pre-invoiced rows. Unreceived or destroyed rows leave both numerator and denominator. The rule matches the business definition, confirmed by supply management.

The calculation had no error. Fixing the code would have changed nothing. The question had to be raised at the definition level, so to management, not to the data team.

03 · Blind spot

The worst case falls out of scope

If all of a supplier's orders stay unreceived over a period, it has no eligible row left. It disappears from the report: no rate, no alert. Option chosen by management: include late orders in the denominator with zero delivered, to show 0 % rather than nothing.

04 · Source

Three families of source anomalies

In parallel, three defects in the ERP → data warehouse chain: over-deliveries posted on the wrong row (29 rows in the week studied), orphan receipts with no order, duplicated rows with swapped quantities.

Flagging an anomaly without measuring its weight is raising an alert without knowing if it matters.

05 · Impact

Measure before fixing

Recomputed with row-level capping, min(delivered, ordered), and orphan receipts excluded. Maximum gap of 1.2 points on a small flow, under 0.1 point on high-volume warehouses. The bugs exist but do not distort the aggregate; the blind spot, on the other hand, was hiding an entire supplier.
