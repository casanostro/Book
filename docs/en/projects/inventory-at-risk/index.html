14 %, then 4 %: the number that survived

How much stock is sleeping in our warehouses? The first answer was spectacular. Before defending it to Finance, I attacked it myself.

First measure (V1)
14.3 %
inventory at risk · vs
After hardening (V16)
4 %

Same stock, same period, a third of the risk. The method had not changed in principle; it had stopped confusing what is sold with what drops out of a file.

Investigation

From a stock snapshot to a defensible number

01 · Question

How much is sleeping?

A simple question nobody had worked through with data: out of about 30 M€ of warehouse stock, what share no longer moves? Without an answer, no provision, no purchasing trade-off, no target.

02 · Method

A sell-through rate, not a black box

Stock snapshot at the start of the period, outflows measured from daily stock decreases over a rolling 12 months, divided by the opening stock. Thresholds: critical below 10 % a month, high up to 20 %, moderate up to 30 %. Then an ABC split on value to know where to act first.

Industry benchmarks put world-class dormant stock around 10 % and the alert level at 15 %. At 14.3 %, the number was plausible: that is exactly what made it dangerous.

03 · Cross-check

Test the model against accounting

Before presenting, an item-by-item match with the accounting valuation. A 4.6 M€ gap. Stock decreases were not only sales: they also absorbed inventory adjustments and breakage, and the scope included items replenished during the year.

A model that has never been tested against an independent source is not validated. It is only consistent with itself.

04 · Correction

Two rules, side by side

Rule A: the original sell-through rate on the tightened scope. Rule B: the same, with opening stock corrected for inventory adjustments. Items that dropped out between two versions are traced one by one with their reason, instead of disappearing silently.

05 · Deliverable

Leave the call to whoever books the provision

A list prioritised by ABC class, both rules shown together with their gap, and a method that can be replayed every month. Choosing the provisioning rule belongs to Finance; my job was to make the gap visible and explained.
